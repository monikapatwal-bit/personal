How I stepped into the PM seat mid-project — owning research, design, and the auction PRD — and helped turn a manual Ariba/Excel process into a shipped, AI-native auction module for Diageo India, now live with measured adoption.

01 — Context

A named client, a fragmented stack, and auctions as Phase 1.

Aerchain is an AI-native enterprise procurement platform. Diageo India partnered with Aerchain to digitise and standardise sourcing — replacing a stack fragmented across SAP ECC, SAP Ariba, email, and Excel calculators.

Auctions were a Phase-1 priority: reverse auctions to procure inputs (ENA, bottles, caps) and forward auctions for scrap sales (glass, PET, assets), with outcomes pushed back into SAP. I owned research, design, and the auction PRD; engineering built it.

02 — The Problem

Manual, clunky, and prone to disruption.

Reverse auctions in Ariba were, in the client's own words, "manual, clunky, and prone to disruptions":

  • Supplier addition was manual and one-by-one; inactive vendors (>3 months) had to be reactivated by hand; Excel mass-upload rarely worked.
  • Vendor login and access failures disrupted live events — alternate IDs created mid-auction, delaying participation 5–15 minutes per vendor.
  • Rank-only visibility; buyers monitored live bids and clarified with suppliers over calls and WhatsApp.
  • Evaluation and reporting were Excel-dependent; savings analysis done manually.
  • No automated SAP sync — duplicated effort, and award data could differ across Ariba, SAP, and email.

Forward auctions (scrap) had no competitive price discovery at all — scrap rates were set over email and manual SAP VK11 condition updates.

Governance was thin: offline negotiations (calls, WhatsApp, email) went uncaptured, retendering was rarely documented with justification, and approvals lived in email rather than the system.

❝

Every disruption traced back to a missing capability, not user error — supplier access, live visibility, SAP sync, and an audit trail that didn't exist.

03 — Research

An as-is process map, a screen-by-screen audit, and the category's own rules.

The redesign sits on three research threads. First, Diageo India's as-is process map — actors, systems, the eight-step Ariba flow, pain points, and audit gaps.

Second, a screen-by-screen audit of Ariba's live buyer and supplier flows (auction creation, pre-bid, live event) that surfaced concrete requirements:

  • Bulk line-item editing — multi-select items and edit together (e.g. increment / decrement price in bulk) instead of one at a time.
  • Vendor-wise price control — set ceiling / floor per vendor, not only per item.
  • Richer lot configurations — item-level and basket-level lots, basket-without-item-breakdown, staggered (phased) lots, and configurable review period / lot-wise duration.
  • Bid management & notifications — a reviewed delete-bid path and email notification / resend controls.

Third, a category scan of SAP Ariba, Keelvar, Coupa, GEP, Jaggaer, and Zycus. Two patterns stood out: mature platforms model a family of auction types × award formats where not every combination is valid; and they use buyer-friendly language — Ariba and Keelvar label the "English" auction as Open / Live and treat Sealed-Bid as a separate event type, because buyers recognise "live competition" faster than academic terms. That directly shaped the plain-language naming in the redesign.

❝

The "Surrogate Bidder" workaround said it all — when buyers bid for suppliers, the supplier experience is the real problem.

05 — How We Built It

Formats, structures, statuses, and the guardrails around them.

Formats × structures. English (Forward / Reverse), Dutch, and Japanese — each running in a List or Package structure. Bidding rules and ceiling / floor apply to English; Dutch auto-decreases to the first acceptor; Japanese decreases in rounds where suppliers must accept or exit.

Create wizard. Five steps — Details → Items → Suppliers → Timing & Rules → Review — with buyer settings (sealed bid / anonymous suppliers), team roles (Owner / Collaborator / Moderator), and T&C gating (Gate View / Gate Entry).

Key capabilities. A decrement / increment rule engine locked at publish; auto-extend; ceiling / floor bid coverage; sealed pre-bids with mass Excel upload; live pause / resume + extend (reason logged); "Save Without SAP Push" hold-for-review; n-level approval before SAP; and analytics — Bid Progression, a tamper-evident Audit Trail, and a Message & Clarification Log.

❝

The rule engine locks decrement / increment logic at publish — the auction can't drift mid-event.

06 — AI

AI where the data supports it — and where it doesn't, yet.

Current release

AI-assisted RFx / auction template completion (subject & description); a Vendor Recommendation Engine that surfaces vendors from historical participation — signals include location, vendor type, prior engagement, and a confidence score; and AI-driven bid-evaluation support (assisted scoring and insights).

Roadmap · Jan–Mar 2026 · not shipped

AI Category Intelligence (Transgraph market benchmarking) and an AI-enabled Negotiation module (simulating multi-round vendor discussions and recommending counter-offers).

Today, historical data powers vendor recommendation only — not price. That boundary is deliberate.

08 — Reflection

What stepping up taught me.

The biggest thing I learned is that domain depth is the prerequisite for influence. I could write the PRD, run the KTs, and make the architecture calls because I understood the problem space — not because I had a PM title. The title came later. The credibility came first.

Being the designer and the PM at once was a real advantage on a small team and a tight timeline: no translation layer between what the user needed and what got specced. What I saw in client calls went into the PRD; what I wrote in the PRD, I designed the next day.

The hardest part wasn't the workload — it was making decisions with incomplete information and not second-guessing them in public. That's the PM skill nobody talks about.
