An auction exists to discover a price. Yet the two tools our buyers had — SAP Ariba and our own first version — both forced the buyer to type that price in, then made them fight the interface to run the event. I redesigned the auction from that contradiction outward: buyers set the bounds, suppliers set the price, and the whole event — for both sides — finally lived in one place.

01 — Understanding the Auction & the Problem

What the module is, and why it needed a redesign.

What an enterprise auction is

An auction lets a buyer put line items in front of invited suppliers and let competition set the price — reverse auctions to buy raw materials, forward auctions to sell scrap. Done well, it replaces weeks of back-and-forth email negotiation with a single, governed, live event.

Why it needed a redesign

Our buyers had two tools, and both fought them: SAP Ariba — the incumbent they were moving off — and our own first version. Four problems ran through both.

  • Buyers were forced to enter the price.On the create screen, both tools made the buyer type in a price — the exact number an auction exists to discover.
  • Suppliers had no way in.Ariba served suppliers so poorly that buyers bid on their behalf — the "Surrogate Bidder" workaround. Our v1 added suppliers one by one, by hand.
  • The live event was unreadable.Rank-only numbers on a dashboard that lagged around ten seconds, with clarifications happening over calls and WhatsApp.
  • One rigid format, six status words.Our v1 ran a single English-reverse format, no lot structure, and showed the same auction as six different labels across the app.

02 — My Approach: Research-Driven Design Strategy

Find the real problem first. Design against it second.

Phase 1 — Uncovering Real Problems

Two research threads: the supplier's day, and how the category actually solves this.

Supplier field research · 17 sessions

9/17away from their desk during live auctions
8/17dictated bids to buyer staff to enter
4/17missed auctions entirely
  1. Suppliers weren't at a desk — 14 of 17 were Android-first, and none used a laptop on the move.
  2. Because they couldn't bid themselves, 8 of 17 dictated bids to buyer staff — the human root of the "Surrogate Bidder" pattern.
  3. Four missed auctions outright; participation was leaking before the event even started.
  4. The pre-bid form was the wall — 28 fields, 30–40 minutes to complete.
  5. Network drops mid-event lost bids, so suppliers hesitated to rely on the app at all.

Category analysis · 6 platforms

  1. Mature platforms (SAP Ariba, Keelvar, Coupa, GEP, Jaggaer, Zycus) offer a family of formats, not one.
  2. Not every auction type works with every award format — which became our compatibility matrix.
  3. Ariba and Keelvar label the "English" auction "Open / Live" — buyers recognise behaviour, not academic names.
  4. Sealed-bid is treated as a separate event model, not a visibility toggle.
  5. Award and optimisation logic — lots, cherry-picking — is where enterprise value concentrates.

Phase 2 — Strategic Design Decisions

Three moves, each aimed at one of the problems above.

  • Buyers set bounds; suppliers set the price.Ceiling and floor replace the forced price field; suppliers bid between them. The single change that removed the most confusing input in both tools.
  • One guided flow — and broken setups made impossible.A five-step wizard with plain-language format cards, backed by a compatibility matrix that blocks structurally invalid auctions before a buyer can build them.
  • A live event you can read at a glance.Real-time rank, bid-coverage bands, and item / supplier activity signals — plus in-platform negotiation, so the event never leaves the tool.

03 — Key Features

Six decisions you can see in the product.

  • Guided 5-step wizardDetails → Items → Suppliers → Timing & Rules → Review — about 13 clicks from blank to launch.
  • Ceiling / floor biddingBuyers set the bounds; suppliers provide the price by bidding between them.
  • Bid-coverage bandsEvery live bid is colour-banded Well Bid / Moderate / Low, so quality reads at a glance.
  • Live activity signalsItem heat (Hot / Active / Cold) and supplier presence (Active / Idle / Inactive).
  • In-platform negotiation1:1 and broadcast, audit-logged — no more clarifying over WhatsApp.
  • Auto-extendA late bid nudges the clock, ending "bid at the buzzer" disputes by design.
And one status model everyone could follow — replacing our v1's six labels

Designs were approved by the anchor enterprise clients; engineering build is in progress — tested outcomes, not production metrics.

06 — Reflection

What stepping up taught me.

The biggest thing I learned is that domain depth is the prerequisite for influence. I could write the PRD, run the KTs, and make the architecture calls because I understood the problem space — not because I had a PM title. The title came later. The credibility came first.

Being the designer and the PM at once was a real advantage on a small team and a tight timeline: no translation layer between what the user needed and what got specced. What I saw in research went into the PRD; what I wrote in the PRD, I designed the next day.

The hardest part wasn't the workload — it was making decisions with incomplete information and not second-guessing them in public. That's the PM skill nobody talks about.
